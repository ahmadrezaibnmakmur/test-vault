# 1. Introduction

Software as a Service (SaaS) firms compete not only through product features but also through the reliability of customer support [1]. Because customers use the service continuously, an unresolved inquiry may interrupt operational work, delay a business transaction, and reduce confidence in the provider. Live-chat records therefore provide more than a count of incoming requests: they show whether a product-related inquiry is resolved at the first interaction or becomes repeated work [2].

First contact resolution (FCR) is a critical-to-quality indicator in this setting. It captures whether a customer receives a complete answer without additional follow-up, handoff, or escalation. Its inverse, non-first-contact resolution (Non-FCR), represents an inquiry that requires additional handling. For customers, this means waiting and repeated explanation. For the support organization, it creates rework through further validation, technical escalation, manual correction, and delayed closure [1,2]. Non-FCR can therefore be treated as a service-process defect rather than only as a collection of isolated complaints.

Repeated follow-up, technical handoff, waiting, and additional validation are also recognizable forms of waste in lean service [3]. Such waste becomes manageable when it is classified, measured, traced to recurring patterns, and linked to corrective action [4]. Lean Six Sigma is suitable for this problem because it connects the lean view of service waste with defect measurement and structured process control [5]. DMAIC prevents improvement teams from jumping directly to solutions before establishing the defect pattern, while PDCA provides an operational loop for implementing, checking, and standardizing issue-level actions.

The empirical context of this study is the live-chat customer-support process of an anonymized SaaS provider. Its 2025 baseline contained 39,414 issue inquiries: 34,675 were resolved at first contact and 4,739 became Non-FCR defects. Thus, 12.02% of all issue inquiries required additional handling. Monthly Non-FCR and SLA-breach rates also fluctuated, indicating that the problem involved both the volume of defects and the stability of the resolution process.

Prior research shows that Lean Six Sigma can improve call-centre resolution performance [5], reduce operational delays [8], and support business-process improvement in customer-service settings [6,7]. However, fewer studies connect product-level stratification, issue-category Pareto analysis, CS-engineering follow-up evidence, and post-improvement control at issue-pattern level in a SaaS environment. This study addresses that gap by converting customer-support logs into a traceable improvement flow from baseline measurement to standardization.

Two research questions are stated explicitly. **RQ1:** How are Non-FCR defects distributed across product and issue-category dimensions in a SaaS live-chat support process, and what dominant root-cause dimensions explain the priority defects? **RQ2:** How does a DMAIC-based improvement process, operationalized through PDCA follow-up and control, relate to early changes in FCR, Non-FCR, DPMO, and SLA-breach performance?

The study contributes a practical measurement and control approach for SaaS support operations. It shows how firms can move from broad concerns about response quality to a defect view that identifies where unresolved inquiries are concentrated, which patterns remain open, and which fixes are sufficiently stable to standardize.
