## 4.2. Do: Implementation of Improvement Actions

In the Do stage, the priority issue categories and patterns identified during Plan were converted into trackable corrective actions. Customer Service documented recurring customer symptoms and incomplete resolutions, while engineering and product teams investigated system behavior, query logic, calculation rules, data consistency, and validation procedures. Each issue pattern was recorded with an action count, fixed count, unconfirmed count, and next decision.

The implementation did not treat a category-level FCR increase as sufficient proof that every underlying problem was resolved. Improvement evidence was maintained at issue-pattern level so that unresolved actions could not disappear inside an average. Corrected patterns were validated using operational follow-up notes, while items lacking confirmation remained open for the next review.

The action evidence covered six dominant categories. Examples included Balance Sheet reporting, Purchase Invoice and Purchase Order processes, Goods Receipt and Item Journal, General Journal and General Ledger, Supplier Payable, Master BOM, and Master Product. Fixes addressed machine logic, process rules, validation steps, and measurement visibility rather than relying only on additional agent effort.

The direct output of the Do stage was therefore an auditable CS-engineering improvement register. It linked each priority defect to a root-cause dimension, corrective action, verification status, and pending decision. The performance consequences of these actions are evaluated in the Check stage.
