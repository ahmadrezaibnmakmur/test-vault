# 3. Methodology

This research uses a single-case applied process-improvement design based on Lean Six Sigma DMAIC [5]. The case is an anonymized SaaS company's live-chat customer-support process, and the unit of analysis is one customer inquiry categorized as an issue. Company and product names are withheld to protect operational confidentiality.

The study uses secondary operational records. January-December 2025 provides the baseline. January-April 2026 contains evidence of improvement activity from Customer Service and engineering follow-up notes, while January-May 2026 Product 1 inquiry records provide the early performance evaluation. Each issue inquiry is treated as one unit with one defect opportunity.

The main variables are total issue inquiries, inquiries resolved at first contact, inquiries not resolved at first contact, SLA-breach status, product category, issue category, issue pattern, and internal follow-up status. Product-level stratification identifies the dominant defect source. Pareto analysis prioritizes issue categories, Fishbone 6M organizes root causes, and PDCA evidence records corrective-action progress.

**Table 1. Operational definitions and calculations.**

| Metric | Operational definition and calculation |
| --- | --- |
| FCR | Inquiry resolved at first contact without follow-up or escalation; FCR rate = FCR inquiries / total issue inquiries. |
| Non-FCR | Inquiry not resolved at first contact and requiring additional handling; used as the main service-process defect. |
| DPMO | Defects per million opportunities = Non-FCR / total issue inquiries x 1,000,000. |
| SLA breach | Non-FCR inquiry exceeding the 24-hour resolution limit; used as an impact and control metric. |
| Issue category | Operational category assigned to an issue inquiry and used for Pareto, root-cause analysis, improvement, and control. |
| Fixed rate | Fixed improvement items / recorded action items for an issue pattern. |

The DMAIC phases were implemented as follows. Define established the process boundary from inquiry receipt to closure and selected FCR as the critical-to-quality indicator. Measure calculated the 2025 baseline and stratified defects by product and issue category. Analyze applied Pareto and Fishbone 6M to dominant Product 1 issues. Improve translated the root causes into PDCA actions during January-April 2026. Control compared the baseline, implementation period, and early evaluation and classified issue patterns for standardization or continued review.

The analysis is descriptive and evidence-supported. It does not claim that all changes in 2026 were caused solely by the PDCA activities because the evaluation period is short and other operational influences may exist. The method's value lies in linking baseline measurement, defect prioritization, root-cause evidence, improvement tracking, and control monitoring in one auditable flow.

Only anonymized operational records were analyzed. No directly identifying customer information was used, and the analysis reports aggregated results.
