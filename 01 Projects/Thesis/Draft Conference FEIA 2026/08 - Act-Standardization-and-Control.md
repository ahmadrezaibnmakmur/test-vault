## 4.4. Act: Standardization and Control of Recurring Issue Patterns

The Act stage converted improvement evidence into operational decisions. A fixed-rate measure was used to distinguish patterns that were ready for standardization from patterns that required further validation or another PDCA cycle. This prevented one-time fixes from being treated as permanent improvements without evidence of completion.

Master BOM, Supplier Payable, Purchase Invoice, and several purchasing and inventory patterns were sufficiently advanced for standardization and recurrence monitoring. General Journal, General Ledger, Release Payment, Balance Sheet, and other moderate-rate patterns remained open for further validation. Segmenting Act decisions in this way preserved accountability for unresolved work.

The findings are consistent with Lean Six Sigma customer-support research that moves from broad service problems to focused process improvements [6-9]. Pareto analysis prevented the project from treating all inquiries equally, while issue-pattern tracking connected customer symptoms to specific product or process fixes. The SaaS context adds an operational control layer because recurring issue categories can be returned directly to joint Customer Service and engineering review.

Practically, SaaS support teams should not rely only on response speed. They need a defect view of unresolved inquiries, a shared tracker between Customer Service and engineering, and a control plan that returns recurring patterns to PDCA. The approach requires no new customer-facing platform; it depends primarily on consistent defect definitions, category-level monitoring, ownership, and disciplined follow-up.

The study remains limited by its single-case scope, internal data, and short early-evaluation period. Category-level improvement is not uniform, external operating conditions may influence the observed changes, and the current analysis does not connect FCR directly to customer satisfaction or retention. Future research should extend the control period, apply statistical process control, and validate the approach across additional SaaS products or firms.
