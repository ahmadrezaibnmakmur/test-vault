# Improving First Contact Resolution in SaaS Customer Support through Lean Six Sigma

**Ahmad Reza^a,1^ and Ardhianiswari Diah Ekawati^a^**

^a^ Industrial Engineering Department, BINUS Graduate Program - Master of Industrial Engineering, Bina Nusantara University, Jakarta, Indonesia

**Abstract.** Software as a Service (SaaS) firms depend on customer support to resolve product-related inquiries quickly and completely, yet inquiries that require repeated interaction, validation, or escalation create rework and delayed closure. This study examines how non-first-contact-resolution (Non-FCR) inquiries can be made visible and reduced through a Lean Six Sigma improvement process. A single-case applied study was conducted in a SaaS live-chat support operation using anonymized operational inquiry records. The 2025 dataset provides the baseline, January-April 2026 records the improvement period, and January-May 2026 provides an early evaluation. The analysis combines product-level stratification, Pareto prioritization, Fishbone 6M, DMAIC, PDCA follow-up evidence, and post-improvement monitoring. The baseline contained 39,414 issue inquiries, including 4,739 Non-FCR defects. Product 1 was the dominant source, and six issue categories contributed 1,794 defects, or 64.42% of Product 1 Non-FCR. Root causes were concentrated in Machine, Method, and Measurement rather than primarily in agent capacity. During the early evaluation, Product 1 FCR increased from 67.93% to 73.74%, the Non-FCR rate decreased from 32.07% to 26.26%, DPMO decreased from 320,684 to 262,586, and SLA breach decreased from 20.21% to 14.56%, while average monthly issue volume increased. The findings show that operational support logs can be translated into an auditable defect-reduction and control process, although the short single-case evaluation limits causal claims.

**Keywords.** Lean Six Sigma, DMAIC, first contact resolution, SaaS, customer support, PDCA
