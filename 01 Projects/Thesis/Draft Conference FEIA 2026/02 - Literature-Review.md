# 2. Literature Review

The theoretical foundation combines SaaS service quality, lean service, and Lean Six Sigma process improvement. SaaS service quality affects continued use because customers evaluate not only software functionality but also the reliability of the service surrounding it [1]. Customer-support inquiries are therefore operational evidence of service quality: recurring unresolved issues can expose weaknesses in product logic, information flow, validation procedures, or coordination between support and engineering.

Lean service provides a waste-oriented view of these problems. Waiting, handoffs, repeated explanation, duplicate validation, and avoidable escalation consume customer and organizational time without creating additional value [3]. Waste classification helps make these patterns visible in service-process data [4]. In a live-chat setting, Non-FCR is a suitable defect representation because it captures whether an inquiry crosses the boundary from a complete first response into additional work.

Lean Six Sigma complements this view with structured measurement and improvement. DMAIC links problem definition, baseline measurement, root-cause analysis, improvement, and control [5]. Pareto analysis prevents the organization from treating every product and issue category as equally important, while Fishbone 6M provides a structured lens for examining Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, and Environment. DPMO translates Non-FCR into a normalized defect indicator, and SLA breach provides an operational consequence measure.

Evidence from call-centre and customer-support research suggests that lean and Six Sigma methods can improve resolution performance, reduce delay, and support focused process redesign [5-9]. This study extends that logic to a SaaS support process where issue categories and engineering follow-up records permit a more granular link between customer symptoms, system or process causes, corrective actions, and control decisions.

PDCA is used within the DMAIC improvement and control stages as the operating mechanism for issue follow-up. Plan identifies priority issue patterns; Do records corrective actions with Customer Service and engineering; Check reviews fixed, unresolved, and recurring patterns; and Act standardizes effective fixes or returns unresolved items to the next cycle. This integration allows the improvement process to remain auditable after the initial diagnosis.
