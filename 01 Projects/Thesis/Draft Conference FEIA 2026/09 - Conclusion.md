# 5. Conclusion

This study demonstrates that Non-FCR in SaaS customer support can be treated as a measurable and manageable service-process defect. Product-level stratification and Pareto analysis answered RQ1: Product 1 was the priority area, and six issue categories contributed 1,794 defects, or 64.42% of Product 1 Non-FCR. Root-cause analysis showed that Machine, Method, and Measurement were more dominant than agent capacity.

The DMAIC-PDCA implementation answered RQ2 by linking diagnosis to corrective-action evidence and early performance monitoring. During the January-May 2026 early evaluation, Product 1 FCR increased from 67.93% to 73.74%, the Non-FCR rate decreased from 32.07% to 26.26%, DPMO decreased from 320,684 to 262,586, and SLA breach decreased from 20.21% to 14.56%. Average monthly inquiry volume simultaneously increased from 735.67 to 810.40.

The principal contribution is not a claim of final causal proof but an auditable improvement mechanism. It connects baseline measurement, defect prioritization, Fishbone root-cause analysis, CS-engineering action tracking, performance evaluation, and Act decisions at issue-pattern level. For comparable SaaS organizations, the approach provides a practical way to make recurring support failures visible and return unresolved patterns to continuous improvement.

Future work should extend the control period, apply statistical process control, connect FCR with customer-satisfaction or retention outcomes, and validate the method across additional products and firms.
