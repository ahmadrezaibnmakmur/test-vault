As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
The Ummah Does Not Need More
Commentary
Muslims today can explain nearly every problem facing the Ummah.
We know why our communities are weak.
We know why our youth are confused.
We know why our institutions struggle.
We know why Muslims remain consumers instead of owners.
And many Muslims can even explain the solution.
But knowing is not the same as building.
The
Rarest
People Today
The rarest people today are not the ones who can identify problems.
They are the ones willing to sacrifice years of their lives solving them.
Many people would rather enter a fully saturated business simply because it feels safer and familiar.
Another restaurant.
Another copied business model.
Not because the Ummah desperately needs it.
But because somebody else already proved it works.
The Gaps We Continue to
Ignore
Meanwhile, entire Muslim communities still lack:
proper Islamic schools
software systems
global Muslim media platforms
community housing solutions
trade schools
manufacturing
technology infrastructure
serious marketplaces
These are not small gaps.
They shape the strength, independence, and future of the Ummah.
Islam Builds People Who Carry
Responsibility
The Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم did not build a generation that only analyzed problems.
He built people who carried responsibility.
Allah سبحانه وتعالى says:
“And help one another in Al-Birr and At-Taqwa (virtue, righteousness and piety)...”
(Surah Al-Ma’idah 5:2)
The Bigger the Problem, the
Greater
the Value
Real builders understand something important:
The bigger the problem you solve, the more valuable your work becomes to society.
Civilizations are not rebuilt through endless commentary.
They are rebuilt by people willing to:
take risks
learn difficult skills
endure uncertainty
carry burdens for years without applause
A Different Type of Muslim
The Ummah does not only need more awareness.
It needs Muslims willing to build solutions despite difficulty.
So ask yourself:
What problem around you is so important that ignoring it would feel like abandoning a responsibility?
