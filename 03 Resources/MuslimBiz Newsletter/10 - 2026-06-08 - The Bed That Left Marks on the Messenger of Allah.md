# The Bed That Left Marks on the Messenger of Allah

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
The Bed That Left
Marks
on the Messenger of Allah
The greatest leaders in history were not known for their arrogance. They were known for their humility.
As a founder grows, gains influence, attracts followers, and achieves success, a new danger emerges. The danger is no longer failure. The danger is the nafs.
At the beginning, many entrepreneurs are humble because they are struggling. They are unknown. They are seeking help, advice, and support.
But success changes the environment around them.
Followers appear.
Praise increases.
People begin to admire them, quote them, and seek their opinions.
The
Danger
of Praise
The problem is not the followers.
The problem is what praise does to the heart.
Shaytan beautifies the praise. The nafs embraces it. Slowly, a person begins to think more highly of himself than he should. What began as confidence becomes self-importance.
The Messenger of Allah صلى الله عليه وسلم said:
"No one will enter Paradise who has even a mustard-seed's weight of arrogance in his heart."
(Sunan Ibn Majah)
The
Humility
of the Greatest
Yet when we look at the greatest of creation, Muhammad صلى الله عليه وسلم, we find extraordinary humility despite being a Prophet, leader, judge, commander, and teacher.
Umar رضي الله عنه began to weep when he saw the Messenger of Allah صلى الله عليه وسلم lying on a mat woven from palm fibers, and the mat had left marks on his side. (Muslim)
Abu Bakr رضي الله عنه feared hypocrisy even though he was among those who were promised Jannah.
Umar ibn Al-Khattab رضي الله عنه would walk the streets at night serving his people and carrying their burdens.
The
Fear
of the Salaf
The closer they came to greatness, the more they feared themselves.
Today many people fear criticism.
The Salaf feared praise.
A Question for Muslim Leaders
What practices can a Muslim leader establish to ensure that increasing influence produces greater humility rather than greater arrogance?
