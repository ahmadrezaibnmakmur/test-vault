The Muslim Founder’s
Accountability
Audit
Business is one of the most complex activities a person can undertake.
Because of this complexity, mistakes are inevitable. A product may be defective without your knowledge.
An employee may make a mistake. A process may fail despite your best intentions.
Islam does not require perfection. But it does require accountability.
Umar ibn Al-Khattab رضي الله عنه said:
"Take account of yourselves before you are taken to account, and weigh yourselves before you are weighed."
(Muḥāsabat al-Nafs li-Ibn Abī Dunyā 2)
The Muslim founder should not wait until the Day of Judgment for his first audit.
He should audit himself regularly.
Five Areas to
Review
1. Employees
Did I pay them fairly and on time?
Did I burden them beyond what was reasonable?
2. Customers
Did I fulfill my promises?
Did I deliver what I sold?
3. Contracts
Were the terms clear and understandable?
Did I knowingly include unfair conditions?
Business
Practices
Under Review
4. Sales & Marketing
Did I disclose known defects?
Did I exaggerate benefits?
5. Business Funding
Did I use riba-based financing?
Did I accept money tied to impermissible conditions?
More Than Business
Questions
These may appear to be business questions.
In reality, they are questions of worship.
Questions of accountability.
Allah سبحانه وتعالى says:
"Then surely, you will be questioned that Day about the delights."
(Surah At-Takathur 102:8)
The Audit That Matters Most
What would change in your business if you reviewed these five questions as seriously as you review your profit and loss statement?
