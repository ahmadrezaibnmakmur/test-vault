# The Missing Metric: Barakah

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
The Missing Metric:
Barakah
Most Muslims don’t realize it… but one of the fastest ways to destroy barakah in business is to believe that disobedience leads to success.
A Muslim starts with good intentions.
Then the compromises begin.
A doubtful product.
Doubtful-based financing to grow faster.
Doubtful marketing that feels wrong, yet is justified.
Hidden contract terms.
Selling what harms people because “the market demands it.”
Slowly, success becomes tied to money instead of obedience to Allah سبحانه وتعالى.
Where Rizq Really
Comes From
But rizq does not come from customers, investors, or algorithms.
It comes from Allah سبحانه وتعالى.
Allah سبحانه وتعالى says:
“And no moving (living) creature is there on earth but its provision is due from Allâh.”
(Surah Hud 11:6)
And He says:
“And if the people of the towns had believed and had the Taqwâ (piety), certainly, We should have opened for them blessings from the heaven and the earth, but they belied (the Messengers). So We took them (with punishment) for what they used to earn.”
(Surah Al-A‘raf 7:96)
The Competing
Mindsets
The modern world teaches Muslims that compromise brings growth.
Islam teaches that barakah comes through taqwa.
These are two completely different paths.
One prioritizes short-term gain.
The other secures long-term increase.
The Example of the
Sahabah رضي الله عنهم
The Sahabah رضي الله عنهم built wealth, traded, and led markets.
But they feared haram more than they feared poverty.
That is why Allah سبحانه وتعالى placed barakah in what they built.
A Different Way to
Measure Success
What would your business look like if you treated barakah
like a metric, similar to conversion rate or profit margin?
Would you still accept doubtful income?
Would you still ignore questionable contracts?
Would you still justify harmful products?
Build for Barakah
When barakah becomes the metric:
You choose obedience over shortcuts.
You prioritize clarity over manipulation.
You build with trust, not just strategy.
Because in the end, growth without barakah is not real growth.
And what is built without it… does not last.
