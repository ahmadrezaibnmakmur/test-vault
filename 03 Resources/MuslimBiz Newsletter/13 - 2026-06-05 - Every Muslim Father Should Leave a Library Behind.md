# Every Muslim Father Should Leave a Library Behind

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
Every Muslim Father Should Leave a
Library
Behind
Most fathers think about leaving behind money, land, a house, or a business.
Few think about leaving behind knowledge.
Yet the Messenger of Allah صلى الله عليه وسلم said:
"When a person dies, all his deeds come to an end except three: ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, or a righteous child who prays for him."
(Sahih Muslim 1631)
Every Muslim father should leave behind a library.
Not because books look impressive on a shelf.
But because knowledge is one of the few things that can continue benefiting people long after its owner has returned to Allah سبحانه وتعالى.
What Every Family Library Should
Contain
A Muslim family library should contain the foundations that help preserve the deen across generations.
This includes:
The Qur'an and its tafsir
Books of hadith
Books of fiqh
Books of aqidah
The seerah of the Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم
The biographies of the Sahabah رضي الله عنهم
The works of the scholars رحمهم الله
Books on family, parenting, and character
Books related to the family's trade or profession
These books become teachers that remain inside the home long after the father is gone.
Write
More
Than You Leave
Books written by others are important.
But every father should also write.
Write about your parents.
Write about your grandparents.
Write about their struggles, sacrifices, migrations, trades, and lessons.
Many families can trace their lineage but cannot tell the story of the people who came before them.
Names survive.
Stories disappear.
A hundred years from now, your descendants should inherit more than your last name.
They should inherit your story.
The Power of
Preserved
Knowledge
Research has consistently found that children raised around books tend to achieve higher educational outcomes throughout life.
Books shape minds.
Libraries shape cultures.
Cultures shape generations.
The scholars of Islam preserved the Qur'an, hadith, fiqh, language, and history through documentation.
Had they relied only on memory, much of that knowledge would have been lost.
The same principle applies to families.
The same principle applies to businesses.
The same principle applies to communities.
Knowledge survives when it is intentionally preserved.
Don't Let
Experience
Die With You
Many people spend decades building knowledge that disappears the moment they die.
A businessman spends forty years understanding customers, systems, negotiations, and leadership.
Then none of it is documented.
A craftsman masters his trade.
Then the expertise disappears with him.
A father learns lessons through hardship and sacrifice.
Then future generations never hear them.
This forces every generation to start from zero.
Strong families preserve knowledge.
Weak families repeatedly rediscover what previous generations already learned.
A Library Is an Institution
Many people see a library as furniture.
Islamic civilization viewed libraries differently.
They were institutions of preservation.
A family library serves the same purpose.
It protects knowledge.
It preserves identity.
It transfers wisdom.
It connects generations that may never meet each other.
The books on the shelf may one day become teachers for grandchildren and great-grandchildren you will never know.
The Question for the Next Generation
A library is not furniture.
It is an institution inside the home.
What knowledge, stories, and lessons would remain of your family if your great-grandchildren could only learn from what you intentionally recorded today?
