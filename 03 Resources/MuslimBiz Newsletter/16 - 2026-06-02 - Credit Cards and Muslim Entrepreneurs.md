# Credit Cards and Muslim Entrepreneurs

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
Credit
Cards
and Muslim Entrepreneurs
One of the calamities of modern business today is the normalization of credit cards among Muslims.
It often begins with a simple justification.
"Everyone uses them."
"My business requires it."
"I can't rent a car."
"I read this fatwa."
Before long, a Muslim founder finds himself signing an agreement that contains an explicit stipulation of riba, hoping he will never have to face its consequences.
The Question Is Bigger Than
Permissibility
To be fair, many contemporary scholars and fiqh councils have discussed this issue extensively.
While some permit credit cards in cases of need if no interest is ever paid, most agree that conventional credit cards contain a contractual stipulation involving riba, as the Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم warned:
“Allah has cursed the one who consumes usury, its client, its witness, and its recorder...”
(Musnad Ahmad)
But perhaps we should ask a deeper question.
Is this really the highest standard a Muslim founder can aspire to?
Have we reached a point where our ambition is simply to remain as close to riba as possible without falling into it?
Driving
Along the Edge of a Cliff
Imagine driving a vehicle along a mountain road where one side ends in a cliff.
Throughout the entire journey, you keep your tires inches away from the edge.
Someone asks whether this is wise.
You respond:
"Don't worry. I guarantee I won't fall."
Most people would not call that wisdom.
They would call it unnecessary risk.
The Future Is
Hidden
From Us
Can you really guarantee that your income will not suddenly stop?
Can you guarantee that you will not lose your existing wealth?
Allah سبحانه وتعالى says:
"Verily Allâh, with Him (Alone) is the knowledge of the Hour, He sends down the rain, and knows that which is in the wombs. No person knows what he will earn tomorrow, and no person knows in what land he will die. Verily, Allâh is All-Knower, All-Aware (of things)."
(Surah Luqman 31:34)
The reality is that we cannot guarantee any of these things.
A founder who says, "I will always pay it off before interest is charged," is making assumptions about a future that Allah سبحانه وتعالى has concealed from him.
Success Does Not Require
Compromise
Many Muslim entrepreneurs have accepted the false idea that compromise is necessary for success.
But true tawakkul is believing that:
Rizq comes from Allah سبحانه وتعالى
Barakah comes through obedience
Nothing abandoned for His sake is ever a loss
The path to success is not through disobeying Allah سبحانه وتعالى.
It is through obeying Him even when the world insists otherwise.
Distance From Haram, Not Proximity To It
A Muslim should not aim to see how close he can get to haram without crossing the line.
He should aim to create as much distance as possible between himself and what displeases Allah سبحانه وتعالى.
That mindset changes decisions.
It changes businesses.
And eventually, it changes societies.
A Higher Standard for Muslim Founders
What would change in the Muslim economy if founders optimized for distance from haram rather than proximity to it?
