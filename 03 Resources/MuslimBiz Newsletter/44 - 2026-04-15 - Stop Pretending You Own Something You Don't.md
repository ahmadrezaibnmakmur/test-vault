# Stop Pretending You Own Something You Don’t

Gmail: https://mail.google.com/mail/#all/19d92cc0a56eea5e

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
Stop
Pretending
You Own Something You Don’t
This is what most Muslims get wrong… dropshipping itself is not the problem. Pretending to own something you don’t own is the problem.
Yesterday we covered why it can be impermissible:
You cannot sell what you do not own.
The Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم said:
“Do not sell that which you do not possess.”
(Sunan al-Nasa’i 4613, Abu Dawud 3503, al-Tirmidhi 1232)
But here’s what most people miss:
The same dropshipping model can be permissible if structured correctly.
Structure 1.
Agent for the Supplier (Wakalah)
You are not the seller.
You bring the customer.
The customer clearly understands they are not buying the product from you.
The supplier owns and delivers the product.
You take a clear, agreed commission.
Structure 2.
Agent for the Customer
The customer gives you the money.
You buy the product on their behalf.
You charge a service fee for your work.
You are using their money, not yours.
They know you are not selling it to them.
Structure 3.
Profit Sharing with the Supplier
The supplier sets a base price.
You sell at a higher price.
You split the profit based on a clear agreement.
Everything must be defined.
No ambiguity.
The Core
Principle
Notice the pattern:
You are no longer pretending to own what you don’t own.
That is the difference.
This deen does not block business.
It forces clarity in how you earn.
What Changes Everything
When ownership, responsibility, and roles are clear:
Trust increases.
Transactions become stable.
And income becomes clean.
So the real question is not whether dropshipping works.
It is whether you are structuring it with honesty… or building it on assumptions that do not hold.
