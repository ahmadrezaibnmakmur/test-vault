# Leaving Your Children Wealthy Is Sunnah

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
Leaving Your Children
Wealthy
Is Sunnah
Most Muslim fathers today spend 40 years building someone else’s machine while leaving nothing their children can continue.
A factory job ends when the worker retires.
A salary disappears when the employee dies.
Then the next generation starts again from zero.
New resume.
New rent.
New debt.
New struggle.
The cycle repeats because nothing tangible was built, owned, documented, or transferred.
Think
Beyond
Your Lifetime
A Muslim father should think beyond surviving his own lifetime.
Not every Muslim will build a massive company.
But every Muslim should aim to leave behind some form of productive asset:
a business
land
property
a workshop
a trade
a network
a skill that feeds generations
Something real.
Something transferable.
Something that allows the next generation to begin from level two instead of restarting at level zero.
The
Guidance
of the Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم
The Prophet صلى الله عليه وسلم said:
“You’d better leave your inheritors wealthy than leave them poor, begging of others.”(Sahih al-Bukhari 5354)
This is not greed.
It is responsibility.
The Trap of Modern
Careers
For many Muslims today, the trap is not laziness, even though that exists too.
It is spending an entire lifetime inside careers that produce income but create no inheritance.
High salaries often create comfortable consumption, but not continuity.
And when the father dies, the expertise dies with him because the children were never trained, involved, or positioned to continue the work.
How
Civilizations
Become Strong
A civilization becomes powerful when fathers plant structures
their children can expand.
This is how merchants, craftsmen, farmers, and scholars historically built stability across generations.
They did not only leave money.
They left systems, skills, relationships, a
nd productive ownership.
Building Beyond Yourself
What would change in the Ummah if Muslim families stopped thinking only about earning income and started thinking about transferring productive ownership?
