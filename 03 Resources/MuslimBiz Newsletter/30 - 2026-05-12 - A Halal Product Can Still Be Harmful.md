# A Halal Product Can Still Be Harmful

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
A Halal Product Can Still Be
Harmful
Most Muslims think the only question in business is:
“Is it technically halal?”
But a civilization is not built on bare minimum compliance.
Technical Halal Is Not the Highest
Standard
A Muslim can sell a technically halal product that still harms people.
A sugary drink.
Ultra-processed food.
Candy built around addiction and endless consumption.
No pork.
No alcohol.
No explicitly haram ingredients.
Technically halal.
But does it make people healthier, stronger, more disciplined, or closer to Allah سبحانه وتعالى?
Or does it slowly weaken them?
The
Difference
Between Halal and Ihsan
This is the difference between halal and ihsan.
A society focused only on:
“What can we get away with?”
will never reach excellence.
Umar ibn al-Khattab رضي الله عنه said:
“We abandoned nine-tenths of the halal fearing that we may fall into the haram.”
This was not weakness.
It was a pursuit of higher standards.
Permissible
Does Not Always Mean Beneficial
Today, many Muslims treat “halal” as automatically meaning good, noble, and beneficial.
But something can be halal while still being:
harmful
excessive
addictive
beneath the standard of excellence
Muslims should not aim to merely build halal businesses.
We should aim to build products that elevate human beings.
The Decline
Begins
Here
Because when an Ummah stops asking:
“What is best?”
and only asks:
“What is permissible?”
its decline has already begun.
Building With Ihsan
What would happen to the Muslim economy if founders built products around ihsan instead of simply technical permissibility?
