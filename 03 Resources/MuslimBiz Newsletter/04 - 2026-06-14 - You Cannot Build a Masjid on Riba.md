# You Cannot Build a Masjid on Riba

As-salamu alaykum
Reza,
You Cannot Build a Masjid on
Riba
You cannot build the Ummah on riba and then wonder where the barakah went.
I recently met a brother from Germany who told me something that disturbed me.
In some areas, he said, masajid were built using bank loans.
Naturally, I asked whether he meant Islamic financing.
He corrected me.
"No, conventional loans."
A Difficult
Question
Whether this is widespread or isolated is not the point.
The point is that it forces us to ask a difficult question.
If we build our masajid, schools, markets, charities, and institutions on foundations that Allah سبحانه وتعالى has forbidden, what exactly are we expecting to grow from them?
Many Muslims speak about reviving the Ummah.
Many speak about building communities.
Many speak about solving our problems.
Yet we often search for solutions using the same systems that contributed to our weakness in the first place.
The
Acceptance
of Du'a
The Messenger of Allah صلى الله عليه وسلم mentioned the man who travels long distances, raises his hands to the sky, and says, "O Lord, O Lord," but his food is haram, his drink is haram, his clothing is haram, and he is nourished by haram, so how can his du'a be accepted?
If purity of earnings affects the acceptance of an individual's du'a, what happens when entire institutions are established upon impermissible foundations?
Foundations
Shape Institutions
A building is not just bricks and concrete.
Every institution carries the values upon which it was established.
The early Muslims built markets, masajid, schools, and entire civilizations. But they understood that obedience to Allah سبحانه وتعالى was more important than speed.
What Are We Building?
The question is not whether we can build faster through compromise.
The question is what kind of civilization we are actually building when compromise becomes the foundation.
